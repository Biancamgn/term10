Progressive overload without micro-plates

Fixed dumbbells mean you can't add 2.5 kg at a time, so you progress through reps and tempo instead:

  1. Hit the top of the rep range on every set, two sessions running.
  2. Then pick one: add a set — or slow the lowering phase to 3 seconds — or move up a weight and restart at the bottom of the range.
  3. Log every session: weight, sets, reps. Without a log there's no overload, just exercise.

The dishes

Beef giniling4 servings · ~32 g each · Week B Sunday
500 g lean ground beef · 2 potatoes, diced · 2 carrots, diced · 1 cup tomato sauce · 1 onion · 4 cloves garlic · soy sauce · salt, pepper
  1. Brown the beef, then drain the fat off — saves about 150 kcal per batch.
  2. Add onion and garlic, cook until soft.
  3. Add tomato sauce, a splash of soy sauce, the potato and carrot. Simmer 20 min until the potato is tender.

The best reheater on this list — holds four days easily and freezes well. Worth making double.

Beef & egg rice bowl2 servings · ~38 g each · Week B Tuesday
300 g ground beef · 1 onion, sliced · 3 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tbsp sugar · 1 tsp grated ginger · 2 eggs · rice
  1. Soften the onion, add the beef and brown.
  2. Add soy sauce, sugar, ginger and a splash of water. Simmer 5 min until glossy.
  3. Spoon over rice, top with a soft-cooked egg.

Cook fresh — the soft egg doesn't survive storage. Fifteen minutes start to finish, and you're home from DIGDACM at 4:00 with nothing after.

Chicken adobo6 servings · ~35 g each · Week A Sunday
1.5 kg chicken thighs · ¾ cup soy sauce · ½ cup vinegar · 1 cup water · 1 head garlic, crushed · 2 bay leaves · 1 tsp peppercorns · 1 tbsp sugar
  1. Brown the chicken skin-down in a dry pot, 5 min — no oil, the skin renders its own. Remove.
  2. Fry the garlic in the rendered fat, 1 min.
  3. Return the chicken. Add soy sauce, water, bay leaves, peppercorns. Simmer covered 25 min.
  4. Add the vinegar and don't stir for 5 minutes — letting it boil off first cooks out the raw sharpness.
  5. Uncover, add sugar, simmer 10–15 min until the sauce coats a spoon. Dump in a cup of frozen veg here.

Genuinely improves by day two, and the acid keeps it safe longer than most dishes.

Chicken teriyaki2 servings · ~35 g each
500 g chicken thighs · ¼ cup soy sauce · 2 tbsp sugar · 1 tbsp vinegar · 1 tsp grated ginger
  1. Airfry the chicken at 200 °C for 20 min, flipping once.
  2. Meanwhile simmer the sauce ingredients 3–4 min until syrupy.
  3. Slice the chicken, toss in the sauce.
Tofu sisig2 servings · ~20 g each
3 blocks firm tofu · 1 onion, diced · 3 cloves garlic · 2 tbsp soy sauce · calamansi · chili optional
  1. Press the tofu 15 min under something heavy — wet tofu steams instead of crisping.
  2. Cube and airfry at 200 °C for 15 min until golden.
  3. Sauté onion and garlic, add tofu, soy sauce, calamansi. Toss 2 min.

An egg on top takes it from 20 g to 27 g.

Sardine pasta2 servings · Week B Thursday portable
200 g pasta · 2 cans sardines in tomato sauce · 4 cloves garlic, minced · 1 onion · 1 cup frozen veg · calamansi · pepper
  1. Boil the pasta, drain, rinse cold — stops the cooking so it doesn't turn to mush overnight.
  2. Sauté onion and garlic. Add frozen veg, 3 min.
  3. Add the sardines with their sauce, break them up, 2 min.
  4. Toss with the pasta off the heat. Calamansi, pepper.

Already sauced and cheaper than tuna. Fine at room temperature — half Thursday, half Friday lunch.

200 g pasta · 2 cans tuna in oil · 4 cloves garlic · 1 onion · 1 cup frozen veg · calamansi · salt, pepper
  1. Boil, drain, rinse cold.
  2. Sauté onion and garlic, add frozen veg, 3 min.
  3. Add the tuna with its oil, break it up, 2 min.
  4. Toss off the heat with calamansi and seasoning.
Egg sandwich~20 g · 5 minutes · Week B breakfast
2 eggs, scrambled · 2 slices whole wheat · pepper

Five minutes instead of fifteen, which is the whole point at 6:45. A leftover giniling scoop in there takes it to ~28 g.

Hard-boiled eggs3 per cook day · both weeks
Cold water · bring to boil · kill the heat · cover 11 min · ice bath

Unpeeled they keep 1 week in the fridge. Peeled and stored in water, 3–4 days — change the water daily. Back of a middle shelf, never the door, where the temperature swings every time it opens.

Storage

  • Everything into containers within two hours of cooking, then straight to the fridge. Don't leave pots cooling on the counter overnight.
  • Rice reheats best with a tablespoon of water and a damp paper towel over it.
  • Bananas: room temperature while green or yellow — the fridge blackens the peel. Once fully ripe the fridge buys another 5–7 days. Keep them away from other fruit, split the bunch into singles, and wrap the crown in cling film for 2–3 extra days. If they're outrunning you, peel, chop and freeze. In Manila humidity expect 4–5 days from yellow to spotty, so buy slightly green.
  • Portions: about ¾ cup cooked rice per meal rather than a full cup, roughly double the protein you'd normally serve.

Sleep reset — before Sept 7

Target: 10:30 PM lights out, 6:00–6:15 wake — about 7.75 hours, which clears the 6:45 breakfast and the 7:30 classes on Mon, Tue and Fri.

Shift in 30-minute steps every 2 days, not one jump. Start at whichever rung matches where you actually are now.

What moves the clock

  • Wake time is the lever, not bedtime. You can't force sleep, but you can force yourself out of bed. Fix the wake time and the bedtime follows within a few days.
  • Morning light within 30 minutes of waking — 10–15 min actually outdoors, not through a window. Strongest signal your body clock has, and it's free.
  • Dim everything after 9 PM. Lamps only, phone brightness down. Light at night pushes the clock later, which is what you're fighting.
  • No caffeine after 2 PM. Half-life is 5–6 hours, so a 4 PM coffee still has a quarter circulating at 10 PM.
  • Keep dinner at 7 PM. Meal timing is a secondary clock signal — consistency helps the shift hold.

What undoes it

  • Sleeping in more than an hour on weekends. Wake by 7:15 Sat and Sun.
  • Studying in bed. Keep the bed for sleep or your brain stops associating it with sleep.
  • Napping after 3 PM. If Thursday wrecks you, nap before 3 and cap it at 25 minutes.

If you've shifted successfully and still can't fall asleep at 10:30 after a couple of weeks, or you're getting the full 8 hours and still exhausted through the day, that's worth raising with a doctor rather than pushing harder on the routine.

07

When it slips

This plan holds about 20 hours of study, 4 gym sessions, 2 prep sessions and a calorie deficit at the same time. Something will give, especially in the first fortnight. Sacrifice in this order:

1stStep goal
2ndA study block
3rdA gym session
—Never sleep. Never protein. These two are what make the rest survivable.
Two things worth rereading in week 3

1540 kcal sits only ~150 above your BMR. That's a legitimate deficit at your height, but it's the floor. Going lower doesn't speed anything up — it costs you muscle, tanks your energy through an 8-hour Thursday, and makes the whole thing collapse. If progress looks slow and you want to cut further, that's the moment to hold steady instead.

Progress will look flat for two-week stretches and then drop suddenly. That's how it works, and it isn't a signal to change anything.
